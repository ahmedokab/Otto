01 · The brain

It starts at the OBD-II port.

Every car sold in the US since 1996 has one, under the dashboard on the driver's side near the pedals. An OBD-II cable runs from it to the FREE-WILi, and Otto talks to the car's computers over CAN (standard since 2008), asking only read-only questions.

  • VIN & vehicle
  • Supported sensors
  • Trouble codes, 4 ways
02 · Engine & ignition

How hard is it working?

Speed, load and timing, sampled twice a second. An unsteady idle or misfire shows up here before you feel it.

  • RPM
  • Engine load
  • Timing advance
03 · Fuel & air

Is the mixture right?

Fuel trims show how hard the computer is correcting. Big corrections point to vacuum leaks, dirty sensors or weak fuel delivery.

  • Fuel trims
  • Intake pressure
  • Air-fuel ratio
04 · Cooling

Running too hot?

Coolant temperature is the one reading that can say "pull over now". Otto watches the peak, not a single blip.

  • Coolant temp
  • Oil temp
  • Intake temp
05 · Battery & charging

Is it charging?

13.5 to 14.7 volts with the engine running means the alternator is doing its job. Less, and you could be stranded.

  • System voltage
  • Engine on / off
06 · Throttle & pedal

What you asked for vs. what you got.

In most modern cars the gas pedal talks to the throttle electronically. Otto compares what you asked for with what the throttle did: the first place to look when a car suddenly loses power or shows a throttle warning.

  • Gas pedal
  • Throttle commanded
  • Throttle actual
07 · Emissions & exhaust

Clean and efficient?

Catalyst temperature and the oxygen sensors around it tell whether the exhaust is being cleaned, and whether you'd pass an emissions test.

  • Catalyst temp
  • O2 sensors
  • Readiness

How it works

Four steps. No guesswork.

  1. 01

    Connect

    OBD-II cable into the port under the driver-side dashboard, other end into the FREE-WILi. Turn the car fully on; Otto finds the car and reads its VIN.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Press Start capture for live readings and every kind of trouble code. Mark the moment something feels wrong.

  3. 03

    Understand

    A health score, what's going well, and what each code means in plain words, checked against your data.

  4. 04

    Fix or find help

    Safe DIY steps first, easiest to hardest. If it's beyond a driveway job, find a shop near you with your report in hand.

Safety first

Built to be safe in your car.

Asks, never tells

Only standard read requests leave the device. Clearing codes, writing settings, resets and reflashing are blocked in code.

Honest about limits

A code points to a condition, not a broken part. Otto says what the data supports, and when a professional is the right call.

Yours to keep

Save a dated report of your car's health after every check: documentation for you, evidence for the shop.
